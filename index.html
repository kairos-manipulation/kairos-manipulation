KAIROS: Keyframe-Aligned Long-Horizon Video Imagination for Robot Manipulation

KAIROS uses semantic keyframes to structure long-horizon robot manipulation, generate future video trajectories, and provide compact future representations for action prediction.

Mohammad Khoshnazar · Zhiyuan Gao · Andrew Melnik · Michael Beetz
Institute for Artificial Intelligence · University of Bremen
Project Overview Watch Videos
KAIROS converts an initial observation and task instruction into semantic keyframes, generates future robot videos between them, and uses the resulting future representation for manipulation.

Overview

Long-horizon manipulation requires understanding not only the final goal, but also the important intermediate states of the task. KAIROS addresses this by representing manipulation progress through semantic keyframes such as contact, partial progress, regrasp, and goal state.

Instead of predicting one long video directly, KAIROS breaks the task into shorter and more meaningful intervals. A video model generates motion between neighboring keyframes, and the resulting future video representation can guide an action model.

Approach

The method follows a simple pipeline: observe the scene, identify task-relevant future keyframes, generate video segments between them, and use the future representation for robot control.

🖼️
Observation Initial image and task instruction
📍
Keyframes Contact, progress, and goal states
🎞️
Video Imagination Motion between semantic anchors
📦
Future Latent Compact representation of predicted motion
🤖
Action Prediction Future-conditioned robot manipulation

Semantic Keyframes

Keyframes capture important task stages and help structure long-horizon manipulation into meaningful steps.

Stable Video Generation

Video generation is performed between neighboring keyframes, where the motion is more constrained and easier to model.

Future-Conditioned Control

The generated future video is encoded into a latent representation that can support action prediction.
